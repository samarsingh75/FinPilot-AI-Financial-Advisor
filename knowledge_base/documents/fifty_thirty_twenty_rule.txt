The 50/30/20 Budgeting Guideline

The 50/30/20 rule is a common budgeting guideline. It is a simple starting point, not a universal requirement and not a law of personal finance. Many people adjust the percentages to fit their income, location, and goals. In expensive cities, or on a lower income, spending 50 percent on needs may be unrealistic.

How it works. After taxes, divide your monthly income into three groups.

Needs, about 50 percent. These are costs you must pay to live and work: rent or housing, basic groceries, utilities, transport to work, insurance, and minimum loan payments.

Wants, about 30 percent. These are choices that improve your life but are not essential: dining out, entertainment, streaming subscriptions, shopping beyond basics, and holidays.

Savings and debt repayment, about 20 percent. This includes your emergency fund, investments, retirement contributions, and extra payments on debt beyond the minimum.

Example. With a monthly take-home income of ₹60,000, the guideline suggests about ₹30,000 for needs, ₹18,000 for wants, and ₹12,000 for savings and debt repayment.

Using the guideline. Sort your last few months of spending into needs and wants, then compare your percentages with the guideline. If your needs exceed 50 percent, look for ways to lower fixed costs or increase income over time. If your wants are high, trimming a few categories may free money for savings. If you save more than 20 percent, you are ahead of this guideline.

Limitations. Some expenses are hard to classify, because a mobile plan can be both a need and a want. Households with high debt payments, dependents, or irregular income may need a different split. Treat 50/30/20 as a framework for thinking, and adapt it to your own situation.
