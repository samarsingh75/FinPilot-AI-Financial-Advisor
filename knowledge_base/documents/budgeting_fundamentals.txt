Budgeting Fundamentals

A budget is a plan for how you will use your money over a period of time, usually one month. It is not a punishment or a list of things you may not buy. A good budget helps you decide in advance where your income should go, so that spending reflects your priorities instead of happening by accident.

Step 1: Know your income. Add up all money you reliably receive each month, such as salary, freelance payments, or interest. If your income changes from month to month, use a conservative figure based on your lowest typical month.

Step 2: Track your expenses. Record what you spend for at least two or three months before setting targets. Most people discover small recurring costs, such as subscriptions or food delivery, that they had not noticed. Grouping spending into categories such as rent, groceries, transport, dining out, and entertainment makes patterns visible.

Step 3: Separate fixed and variable expenses. Fixed expenses, such as rent, loan instalments, and insurance premiums, stay about the same every month. Variable expenses, such as groceries, fuel, and shopping, change with your choices. Variable expenses are usually where you have the most room to adjust.

Step 4: Set category limits. Choose a monthly limit for each spending category, based on your tracked history and your goals. Start with realistic limits. A budget that is too strict is difficult to follow and is often abandoned.

Step 5: Review and adjust. Compare actual spending with each limit at the end of the month. If a category is over its limit again and again, either the limit is unrealistic or the spending habit needs attention. Update the budget as your income and life circumstances change.

Savings rate is a useful summary number. It is your net savings divided by your income, shown as a percentage. For example, if you earn ₹60,000 and spend ₹48,000, you save ₹12,000, which is a savings rate of 20 percent.
