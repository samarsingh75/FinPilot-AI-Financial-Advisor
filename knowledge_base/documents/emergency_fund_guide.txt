Emergency Fund Guide

An emergency fund is money set aside only for unexpected and necessary costs, such as a sudden medical bill, an urgent home repair, or a period without income after losing a job. Its purpose is to prevent you from borrowing at high interest rates or selling investments at a bad time when something goes wrong.

How much should you save? A commonly suggested target is three to six months of essential living expenses. Essential expenses include rent, groceries, utilities, transport to work, insurance premiums, and minimum loan payments. They do not include holidays, dining out, or shopping. If your income is irregular, you work in an uncertain field, or others depend on your income, a larger fund of six to twelve months may be more comfortable.

Example: if your essential expenses are ₹30,000 per month, a three-month fund is ₹90,000 and a six-month fund is ₹1,80,000. Reaching this amount can take time, so begin with a small first milestone, such as one month of expenses, and build from there.

Where should it be kept? An emergency fund should be easy to reach and should not lose value suddenly. Many people use a separate savings account or a liquid deposit. Keeping it apart from your daily spending account lowers the temptation to use it for non-emergencies. The fund is not meant to earn high returns. Safety and quick access matter more than growth.

How to build it: set a fixed amount to move into the fund right after you receive your income. Automatic transfers help make this a habit. Direct unexpected money, such as a bonus or a tax refund, towards the fund until the target is reached.

After using the fund, rebuild it. Treat refilling it as a priority before returning to other optional savings goals.
