Planning Savings Goals

A savings goal is a specific amount of money you want to have by a specific date. Clear goals are easier to reach than a vague wish to save more. A good goal states the amount, the deadline, and the purpose, for example: save ₹1,20,000 for a laptop and travel within twelve months.

Short-term, medium-term and long-term goals. Short-term goals, up to about one year, include a holiday or a new phone. Medium-term goals, from one to five years, include a down payment or higher education. Long-term goals, beyond five years, include retirement. The time available affects where the money should be kept. Money needed soon should be kept safe and easy to access, because there is little time to recover from losses.

The basic calculation. Divide the goal amount by the number of months available. To save ₹50,000 in six months, you need about ₹8,333 per month, assuming no interest is earned. This straight-line figure is a quick feasibility check. Compare it with the amount you currently save each month. If your current monthly savings are lower, you can extend the deadline, reduce the goal, increase income, or cut some variable expenses.

Making the plan work. Pay yourself first: move the monthly saving amount into a separate account as soon as income arrives, rather than saving whatever is left at the end of the month. Track progress each month. Give each goal its own name or account, so money for one goal is not accidentally spent on another.

Be realistic. Choose a monthly saving amount that you can maintain. Saving slightly less every month for a longer time is usually more successful than an aggressive plan that fails after two months. Review the goal whenever your income or expenses change significantly.
